Introduction to PCB Production

The electronics manufacturing process involves assembling electronic components and parts through electronic and mechanical assembly to create products that meet design requirements. Printed Circuit Boards (PCBs) are the backbone of most electronic devices, providing the necessary electrical connections between components.

PCB Production Workflow

1

Design Preparation

  • Design Files: Process begins with design files, typically in SVG format for front copper and edge cuts
  • File Conversion: SVG file is converted into G-code using Mods CE (Community Edition)
  • Design Review: Check PCB pad design, trace width, and component locations for defects
2

Material Selection

  • Types of PCBs: Single-sided, double-sided, multi-layer, and flexible boards
  • Substrate Materials: Choice of substrate material is crucial (FR1 to FR4)
3

PCB Fabrication

  • Milling: G-code file controls CNC milling machine to engrave circuit traces
  • Drilling: Holes for through-hole components are drilled as needed
  • Cleaning: Board is cleaned to remove dust and debris
4

Component Assembly

  • Solder Paste Application: Applied using a stencil
  • Component Placement: Surface-mount components placed using pick-and-place machine
  • Reflow Soldering: Board passes through reflow oven
  • Through-Hole Assembly: Components inserted and soldered
5

Testing and Finalization

  • Functional Testing: Board is powered and tested for correct operation
  • Documentation: Comprehensive Bill of Materials (BOM) and schematics maintained

Types of PCBs

Single-Sided PCBs

One layer of substrate with a conductive copper layer on one side. Components are mounted on one side, with traces on the opposite side.

Double-Sided PCBs

Copper layers on both sides of the substrate, allowing for more complex circuits.

Multi-Layer PCBs

Multiple copper layers separated by insulating material, used for advanced electronics.

Flexible PCBs

Made from flexible materials, used in applications where the board needs to bend.

PCB Substrate Materials: FR1 to FR4

FR1

Paper + Phenolic Resin

Properties:

  • Fire retardant
  • Good mechanical strength
  • Low cost
  • Easy to handle

Applications:

Single-layer PCBs, low-cost consumer electronics

FR2

Cotton Paper + Phenolic Resin

Properties:

  • Enhanced over FR1
  • Still low cost
  • Suitable for less demanding applications

FR3

Woven Fiberglass + Epoxy Resin

Properties:

  • Improved electrical properties
  • Higher temperature resistance (up to 170°C)
  • Lower loss tangent

Applications:

Legacy industrial electronics, oscilloscopes, missile guidance systems

FR4

Woven Fiberglass + Brominated Epoxy

Properties:

  • Excellent electrical and mechanical properties
  • High temperature resistance (130–180°C)
  • Low loss tangent
  • Self-extinguishing
  • High flexural strength

Applications:

Industry standard for most modern electronics, including consumer, automotive, and aerospace products

PCB Milling Tools & Bits

V-Shape Engraving/Isolation Bits

Common Angles: 10°, 20°, 30°, 45°, 60°

Main Feature: V-bit angle determines width and depth of cut

Application: Isolation routing to separate copper traces

Purpose: Avoids chemical etching by mechanically removing unwanted copper

Drill Bits (0.3mm to 1.2mm)

Main Feature: Defined by diameter, not cutting angle

Application: Drilling via holes and component lead holes

Size Selection: Based on pin diameter of components

Purpose: Essential for electrical connections between layers

Spiral Drill Bits (1.05mm to 1.3mm)

Application: Drilling larger holes for mounting screws or connector pins

Purpose: Supports mechanical assembly and interface connectors

Requirement: Used where strong, stable holes are needed

Flat End Mill Bits

Main Feature: Flat tip, various diameters available

Application: Cutting PCB shape and removing large copper areas

Purpose: Milling straight, flat-bottom paths for board outlines

Function: Defines board size or cuts individual PCBs from panel

Wegster PCB Milling Machine

The Wegster PCB milling machine is a compact desktop CNC designed specifically for rapid PCB prototyping. It allows users to fabricate circuit boards by milling copper-clad boards instead of traditional chemical etching.

Setting Up the Wegster CNC Machine

Connect via USB

Use the USB cable to connect the Wegster CNC machine to your laptop. Ensure the cable is securely plugged in and the machine is properly powered on.

Switch On the Machine

Locate the power switch on the machine and turn it on. The machine will initialize, and indicators may light up to show it is receiving power.

Open Wegster App

Launch the Wegster controlling software or designated app on your laptop. The software will search for the device if it is not already connected.

Device Detection

After connecting the USB cable, the software will display a message that the device is detected. A blue LED on the machine will start blinking, indicating it is ready.

Milling Process

0.4 mm Flat Endmill

Used for milling wider traces or removing larger areas of copper. Suitable for roughing out the board or cutting the main outline.

0.1 mm 20° V Bit

Most commonly used bit in the lab for milling PCB traces. Allows for very fine, precise isolation of traces due to its small diameter and sharp tip.

Setting Origin and Auto Level

  1. Place the PCB board securely on the machine bed
  2. Check autolevel with the bit - manually jog the Z axis to bring the bit close to the board surface
  3. Set the origin by pressing the "0" button when the bit touches the board
  4. Run autolevel measurement to ensure consistent milling depth
  5. Begin the milling process

Exporting and Preparing PCB Design

1. Export Front Copper Layer as SVG

After completing your PCB design in KiCad, export the front copper layer as an SVG file.

2. Export Edge Cut Layer as SVG

Similarly, export the edge cut (board outline) layer as a separate SVG file.

3. Open Mods CE Website

Go to the Mods CE website and open the appropriate workflow for PCB milling.

4. Process Front Copper Layer

Upload the front copper layer SVG file into Mods CE. Set the bit angle to 45 degrees and the offset to 10. Click Send Calculate to generate G-code.

5. Process Edge Cut Layer

Upload the edge cut SVG file and select the mill outline 1/32 option. Generate G-code for the edge cut.

Final Output

From testing, it was determined that the minimum spacing between traces should be at least 17 mils and the trace widths should be a minimum of 10 mils.

PCB Design Experience: Mini Continuity Tester

"Great innovations often begin with solving the simplest problems."

Introduction

During our work with printed circuit boards (PCBs), we frequently needed to check for continuity between tracks and wires. Searching for a multimeter every time was inconvenient. Inspired by this challenge, we decided to design a simple, compact, and user-friendly tool: a Mini Continuity Tester.

Design Process

1. Schematic Design

We began by creating a schematic using KiCad. The schematic included:

  • Battery: To provide power (coin cell or AAA battery)
  • Resistor: To limit current and protect the LED
  • LED: To visually indicate continuity

2. PCB Layout

After finalizing the schematic, we moved on to the PCB layout. We carefully placed the components to ensure a compact and efficient design. The layout included:

  • Battery holder footprint
  • Resistor and LED placement
  • Test probes or pads for easy probing

3. 3D View

KiCad's 3D viewer allowed us to visualize the final product before fabrication. This helped us ensure that all components would fit properly and that the design was user-friendly.

Assembly and Testing

We soldered the components onto the PCB:

  • Battery holder
  • Resistor
  • LED
  • Test probes

After assembly, we tested the device by probing various points on our PCBs. The LED lit up whenever there was continuity, confirming a successful connection.

Conclusion

This project was a great learning experience in PCB design, fabrication, and problem-solving. The Mini Continuity Tester is now a handy tool in our lab, saving us time and effort during PCB assembly and troubleshooting.
